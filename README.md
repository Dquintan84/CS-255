# CS-255-System-Analysis-and-Design

DriverPass Project Reflection

1. Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?
2. 
The client was DriverPass, a company that wanted to help students prepare for their driving tests. They needed a web-based system where students could take online classes, complete practice tests, purchase training packages, and schedule driving lessons. The system also needed to help employees manage appointments, instructors, vehicles, and student information.

3. What did you do particularly well?

I think I did a good job identifying the system requirements and keeping the design simple and organized. I was able to break down the different functions of the system and show how they would work using UML diagrams. I also made sure to consider both the students and employees who would be using the system.

3. If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

If I could revise one part of my work, I would improve my UML diagrams. Although they show how the system should work, I think I could make some of the relationships and processes clearer. I would also spend more time reviewing the diagrams to make sure they are easy for someone without a technical background to understand.

4. How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user's needs when designing?

I used the information provided by DriverPass to identify what students and employees needed from the system. I then used those requirements to create diagrams showing how users would interact with the system. Understanding the user's needs is important because even a system that works perfectly from a technical standpoint can fail if it doesn't solve the problems it was designed for.

5. How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?

My approach to designing software is to first understand the problem and what the client is trying to accomplish. From there, I break the system down into smaller parts and figure out how everything should work together. In the future, I would continue using UML diagrams, gathering requirements, and reviewing designs before starting development. I think taking the time to plan things out can prevent problems later in the process.
